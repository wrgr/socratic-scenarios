Operational Procedures & Parameters:** * **Aerosol Generation (General):** * Aerosol is transported by a carrier gas (Fig. 1-2). * Aerosol undergoes transportation and refinement (Fig. 1-3). * Aerosol is focussed (Fig. 1-4) and then deposited (Fig. 1-5). * The substrate is translated by computer control (Fig. 1-6). * **Ultrasonic Atomisation (Section 2.1):** * **Ink Viscosity:** Limited to inks with viscosities in the range of 1-10 cp. * **Droplet Sizes:** Typically 2-5 µm, low dispersity. * **Mechanism:** Transducer oscillates in a transfer medium (e.g., water), wave propagates to an ink-filled vial, forming standing waves and large peaks from which small droplets are ejected. Positive pressure drives aerosol to the deposition head. * **Process Tailoring:** Atomisation parameters and ink formulation can be tailored to modify droplet size and distribution to minimize overspray. * **Pneumatic Atomisation (Section 2.2):** * **Ink Viscosity:** Tolerant of 1-1000 cp (datasheets suggest a conservative 1-500 cp). * **Mechanism:** Carrier gas accelerates across ink supply channel, creating reduced static pressure that draws ink. Topmost ink layer is sheared into polydisperse droplets. * **Refinement (Virtual Impactor):** Aerosol is transported to a virtual impactor, where a region of stagnant flow separates droplets based on inertia (Fig. 4). Small, low-inertia droplets are ejected radially (often collected or vented) to the major flow, while large, high-inertia droplets pass through to the deposition head. * **Focussing and Deposition (Section 3):** * **Aerosol Transfer:** Transferred to the deposition head by a carrier gas flow (Fig. 5-1). * **Focussing Mechanism:** Uses a virtual and physical nozzle, with a secondary sheath gas flow (Fig. 5-2) constraining and constricting the aerosol (Fig. 5-3). * **Stand-off Distance:** Consistent deposition at nozzle-substrate offsets of 1-5 mm. * **Deposition Geometry Control:** Achieved by manipulating the aerosol flow rate relative to the sheath gas flow rate (focussing ratio). *

Safety & Challenges (Implicit & Explicit):** * **Nozzle Clogging:** Reduced by sheath gas, but still a concern with certain ink formulations (e.g., high concentration graphene inks). * **Overspray:** Can lead to small satellite deposits, implying a need for careful process control or cleanroom environments to prevent contamination. * **Material Compatibility:** Interplay between surface tension, viscosity, volatility, density with aerosolization methods presents challenges. * **Evaporation Control:** Inadequate control can lead to insufficient inertia for impaction, resulting in particles emitting into the atmosphere rather than depositing on the substrate. * **Ink Formulation:** Lack of a fundamental understanding and process models (like a "Z number" for inkjet) makes initial formulation and optimization highly empirical and time-consuming. * **Substrate Interactions:** Sheath gas interaction with liquid or powdered previous deposits can be a challenge. Wetting characteristics of the substrate are critical and can be challenging to control. * **Cracking & Peeling:** Thermal sintering can cause interconnect cracking (due to CTE differences) and peeling (due to residual stresses) in multi-layered deposits. * **Resolution Limitations:** AJP is limited compared to lithographic processes for extremely fine features and for very short track lengths (<100 µm). Bulges at track ends can limit print resolution. * **Biological Applications:** Low cell viability (87% compared to 97% control) and inability to control cell migration post-deposition are significant challenges. System variability and slow processing times are hurdles for mass production in biology. * **Environmental Factors:** Air pollutants, humidity, and ambient temperature can impact deposit quality and functional characteristics. * **Industrial Adoption:** Hurdles include slow processing times, lack of parallel deposition in current configurations, empirical development, and system variability. Improved understanding through process modeling is needed to decrease development timescales and drive down cost.
